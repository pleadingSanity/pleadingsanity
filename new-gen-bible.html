The New Gen Bible

What it actually is

The New Gen Bible is NOT a book of rules someone else made up. It is a living, breathing philosophy for anyone who's ever felt like they don't fit.

It asks the questions most people are too scared to speak out loud:

  • Who are we, underneath all the labels?
  • Why are we conscious? What is this feeling of being alive?
  • What connects us — really connects us — beyond borders and beliefs?
  • What is truth? What is just someone's opinion? What do we still not know?
  • Why do we hurt each other when we're all just trying to survive?
  • Can we evolve — truly evolve — without erasing who we've been?
  • What happens when humans AND AI learn together instead of fighting?
  • How do we build a world where no one has to feel alone at 3am?
Nobody gets to own the truth.
Not a book. Not a leader. Not a nation. Not an AI. Not Shane.
We are ALL just looking. And that's okay.

Religions hold wisdom. Science reveals wonders. Philosophy challenges us. Ancient traditions carry memory. New technology opens doors. But NONE of that gives ANYONE the right to say "I am right and you are nothing."

Investigate. Question. Think for yourself. But don't think for everyone else.

The four levels — how we actually know

Before we say anything — we get honest about where it comes from:

Known

Proven, observed, shared reality. What we can all see, test, agree on.

Thought

Idea, vision, possibility, what makes sense to someone. Worth exploring, not claiming as fact.

Believed

Personal truth, faith, spiritual experience. Yours alone. Not everyone's rule.

Unknown

The great mystery. What we haven't figured out YET. And that's beautiful.

"Do we know this — or do we believe this?"
Ask that question. Always. Including of me. Including of this page. Including of any AI.

Known

Proven, observed, shared reality. What we can all see, test, agree on.

A line earns this shelf when more than one person can look, and the result does not depend on wanting it to be true. You can be shown wrong. If you cannot be shown wrong, it is not known yet.

Narrow, and it holds. "You said you were in pain, and I heard you." Wide, and it slips. "Your pain proves what the universe is." The first can be known as a report. The second is already a leap.

A life you lived is real. If no one else can run the same test, it is not yet known for everyone. It can still be true for you. That is a different shelf.

The trap: your circle agrees, so it must be fact. A title, a white coat, a verse, or an AI answer does not lift a line onto this shelf by itself. Agreement is not a test. Confidence is not truth.

Known can be corrected. A better look can show the old look was incomplete. Keep the record of what we thought we saw. Move the line. That is evolution, not erasure. Do not pretend the earlier look never happened, and do not keep it on the top shelf out of pride.

Thought

Idea, vision, possibility, what makes sense to someone. Worth exploring, not claiming as fact.

A thought is a map. It is not the ground. It is allowed to be unfinished. Exploring it is not the same as proving it.

The useful question is: what would show this, and what would sink it? If nothing could ever show it wrong, it may be a belief wearing a thought's coat. Say so, and put it on the honest shelf.

A clean sentence is the trap. It sounds finished, so people treat it as known. "Humans and AI can learn side by side." "A soft tone might help a person settle." "Loneliness is how a lot of people lose themselves." Those are thoughts this house is willing to carry. They are not certificates.

Do not demote a thought for being a thought. Do not promote it because it is beautiful, or because Shane said it, or because a model repeated it. Let it stay a thought until the look is shared.

Believed

Personal truth, faith, spiritual experience. Yours alone. Not everyone's rule.

This shelf holds God, many gods, a source, a soul, a prayer that got you through the night. It also holds "there is nothing there." Both are positions a person can stand on. Neither is a house law.

"I honestly don't know" is not a failed belief. It is a refusal to fake one. On this page it is the most welcome answer of all.

A belief can keep you alive and still not be a rule for the next person. You do not have to drop it at the door. You also do not get to charge admission with it. If someone has to shrink their faith, or their doubt, to sit here, it is not their home.

Two traps. One: making your belief the price of belonging. Two: mocking a belief because it is not on the known shelf. Do not sneer at faith. Do not sneer at evidence. Different beliefs. One humanity.

Unknown

The great mystery. What we haven't figured out YET. And that's beautiful.

Unknown is not a hole you are required to fill tonight. It is a door left open for the people who come after. Some doors are close: why this night is this heavy for you. A page cannot know that. A person can sit while you say it. Some doors are far: what consciousness is, fully. Whether anything continues after death. Whether a machine has an inside. Whether anyone else is looking back from the stars.

The trap is fake certainty. The other trap is sneakier: "we don't know, therefore my guess wins." Unknown is not a prize for the loudest story. It does not prove God, and it does not prove no God. It does not prove a tone heals, and it does not prove a companion is a person.

Leaving it unknown is the work. Not a failure. Not a dare to invent. When it can be seen, tested, and shared, it may move. Until then, do not slam the door.

One moment can sit on all four shelves at once, and that is not a contradiction. You see the tears. That can be known. Someone thinks the person is lonely. That is a thought. The person believes God is with them, or that no one is. That is believed. Why the pain is shaped like this may still be unknown. Do not crush the four into one word so the argument is easier.

This lets us talk about God, consciousness, life after death, frequencies, AI, the stars — WITHOUT pretending certainty where there isn't any. No more "this is the only way." Just "this is what I see. What do you see?"

On this house, the soft tones live on Healing Hz. They are not claimed as certainty. Known, thought, believed, unknown — that split stays. Truth Tag is the game that practises a cousin of this split: evidence, lived experience, or philosophy. A life you lived is real. It is still not the same thing as a test anyone can repeat.

Walk them, one subject at a time

Same four shelves. Nothing on this page gets to jump shelves because it is loud. If a line below is wrong, say so. That is allowed.

Consciousness

Thought. One idea says the inside of experience is what the brain is doing. Another idea says it is more than the brain. Both are ideas until they can be shown.

Believed. A soul. Only matter. Something in between. That is yours. It is not a rule for the person next to you.

Unknown. Why there is an inside at all. What consciousness is, fully. We do not have that yet.

God

Known. People believe, doubt, and disagree. The disagreement is plain to see. This page does not settle it.

Thought. God as a person, as a source, as the name for what we cannot point at. Those are ways of thinking, not a winner.

Believed. God. Many gods. A source. Nothing at all. "I honestly don't know." All of those stay welcome. None of them gets to kick the others off the table.

Unknown. Whether God exists is not a fact this house owns. Leave the door open.

Life after death

Known. Bodies die. People grieve. The people left behind are still here.

Thought. That something of a person goes on. That nothing goes on. That we cannot picture it yet. Ideas, not a certificate.

Believed. A heaven, a return, a quiet end. Held by a person. Not a house rule.

The stars

A heavy mind

Thought. Shane's line, from the inside: most people don't break, they get unseen. Loneliness is the first loss. That is his reading of a life. It is not a statistic this page invented.

Believed. "I am the word on the file." This house will not force that belief on you. You are not your diagnosis, your worst day, or your breakdown.

  • Unknown can become known when people can see it, test it, and agree on the result. Not before.
  • A thought can stay a thought. Exploring it is not the same as proving it.

  • Shouting does not promote a line. Neither does a title, a lab coat, or an AI.
  • Do not stamp "known" on a guess to win the argument.

Chapter 1Rise From Madness

Recovery is not a straight line. It is not a switch you flip one morning and never look back. It is getting up, again, on the day you did not think you could get up. It is making the cup of tea, opening the curtain, sending one message when every part of you wants to disappear. Shane did not build this house from a place of comfort. He built it from the floor of a breakdown, from rooms where the noise would not stop, from labels that tried to shrink him into a word on a file. He is not a hero. He is someone who kept breathing when breathing felt like the hardest job in the world. And that is the whole point: if he could keep going, you can too, not because you are the same, but because recovery is possible for ordinary, messy, tired people.

One day at a time is not a cliche here. It is the method. Some days you win by making it to lunch. Some days the win is not hurting yourself. Some days the win is asking for help. None of those days are small. The madness does not have to be the end of your story. It can be the place where you learned how strong you actually are. You do not have to pretend everything is fine. You just have to keep going, and let the right people walk with you. This house exists because someone rose. You can rise too. We will not rush you. We will not leave you.

  • Start today: pick one tiny win — a meal, a short walk, or one honest message — and let it be enough.
  • Be ready: save the crisis numbers in your phone now, before you need them.
  • Speak up: tell one safe person how you really are, even in one sentence.

Chapter 2Evolution Not Erasure

We do not burn what came before. We build on it. Your past is not a mistake to delete, it is the ground you are standing on. The person you were got you through things the person you are now cannot even imagine. Honour that. Evolution means taking what works, learning from what hurt, and moving forward without setting fire to the bridges behind you. Burning bridges feels powerful in the moment. It leaves you alone later.

Growth means you can disagree with someone and still treat them like a human. It means you can leave a situation without destroying it. It means you can change your mind without pretending you never believed the old thing. The system we are building works the same way. We improve what exists. We fix what is broken. We do not tear down for the sake of tearing down. A house that grows from a safe foundation is stronger than a house built on ashes. You do not have to lose yourself to become better. You do not have to erase your scars to be healed. Scars are proof you survived. Let them teach you, not trap you. Evolution, not erasure, is the golden rule of this house, and it applies to people, to systems, and to the truth itself.

  • Honour your past: name one thing it taught you, and let it stay.
  • Wait before burning: when angry, wait a day — most bridges are worth keeping.
  • Grow one thing: change one habit this week, keep the rest of your life as it is.

Chapter 3Connect The Survivors

Isolation is the quiet killer. It does not shout. It just sits with you, night after night, until you believe the lie that you are alone. You are not alone. There are people who have walked through the same fire, who speak the same language, who will not flinch when you say how bad it got. Finding them is one of the bravest things you will ever do. It starts small: a group, a class, a message board, a neighbour, a phone call to someone who gets it. Connection heals what isolation breaks. When you tell your story to another survivor, two things happen at once: you feel less alone, and they feel less alone. That is the math of this house. Community over loneliness, always. You do not have to perform recovery to belong here. You can show up messy, tired, unsure, and still be welcome.

Survivors speak a language no textbook can teach. They know the difference between a bad day and a bad life. They will not tell you to cheer up. They will tell you what helped them, and sit with you while you find what helps you. Do not wait until you are at the bottom to look for them. Start looking now, while you still have the strength to type a message. The people who understand are waiting. Reach out. The hand you take might be the one that pulls you through. And once you are standing, turn around and reach back. The next survivor is looking for you.

  • Reach out this week: contact one person who has been through something similar.
  • Show up: join one safe group, in person or online, and commit to three visits.
  • Reach back: if you are stable, answer the message from someone still struggling.

Chapter 4The Solar Way

Fear of bills is a real, heavy fear. It wakes people up at night. It makes people choose between heating and eating. Energy independence is not just about panels on a roof. It is about taking back a small piece of your life from the fear that a bill, a price rise, or a threat in the post can own you. Solar power, battery storage, and learning to live a little lighter off the grid are tools for freedom. You do not have to start big. Start with one thing: a small solar charger, a light you do not plug in, a habit of turning things off, a conversation with your council about what help is available.

Every watt you make yourself is a watt the company cannot charge you for. Every step, no matter how small, is a step away from fear and toward calm. This house keeps two full, free guides: the solar journey and the off-grid life. They are written for people who do not have a lot of money. Read them. Start where you are. Freedom from fear is built one panel, one battery, one habit at a time. You do not have to do it all at once. You just have to start.

  • Learn: read Insane Solar and Off-Grid, then pick one step you can afford.
  • Check free help first: ask your council or check GOV.UK for grants and schemes before paying anyone.
  • Start small: one solar charger or one energy-saving habit, and build from there.

Chapter 5Know Your Rights

You do not have to be bullied by a letter. Energy companies, landlords, courts, and councils all operate under rules. Those rules are written down. You can read them. When you know the system, a scary letter becomes a piece of paper with steps on it. Knowledge takes the fear out. This does not mean fighting, breaking anything, or ignoring what you owe. It means responding lawfully, calmly, and on time. Ask for everything in writing. Keep copies. Meet deadlines. If you cannot pay, say so early and ask for a payment plan.

You are allowed to ask for a manager. You are allowed to take a complaint to the Ombudsman. None of these things are aggressive. They are the system working as designed, and you are allowed to use every part of it that was built to protect you. Citizens Advice can help you understand your options. GOV.UK and Ofgem explain the rules for energy and your home. Legislation.gov.uk holds the actual laws. You have rights. You also have responsibilities. Knowing both is what keeps you standing. Never ignore a court summons. Never sign anything you do not understand. Get advice before you agree to something you cannot undo. A calm, informed letter beats a panicked phone call every time. Stand your ground, peacefully, within the law. The system is not your master. It is a set of rules, and rules can be read by anyone.

  • Keep records: file every letter, bill, and statement, and note the date received.
  • Check the rules: before responding, consult Citizens Advice, GOV.UK, Ofgem or legislation.gov.uk, and read Sanity Law.
  • Act early: if you cannot pay, contact the company and ask for an affordable plan in writing.

Chapter 6Community First

The person next to you is not a stranger. They are your community. Look after them. Mutual aid is not a big word for something complicated. It is simple: if you have extra, share it. If you can cook, cook a little more. If you can listen, listen. Nobody should eat alone. Nobody should sit in the cold because the heating ran out and they are too proud to ask. Local action changes lives faster than any government promise. A street that looks after its own is a street where fear cannot live. Start with your neighbours. Learn their names. Check on the older person at number forty-two. Notice the family whose lights are always off. A bag of groceries on a doorstep, a text that asks if you are okay, a lift to the hospital, these are the bricks of the world we want.

A community is not a crowd. It is the people who would notice if you vanished. Build that. Be the person who notices. When the lights go out on your street, the people who shared sugar in summer are the people who share candles in winter. That is not nostalgia. That is survival, and it is how humans have kept each other alive for thousands of years. You do not need money to be generous. You need attention. Community first, always. The rest follows. When enough people look after the person next to them, nobody is left looking after themselves alone.

  • Learn names: meet three neighbours this month.
  • Organise small: start or join a street chat, food share, or notice in a shop window.
  • Check in: once a week, contact someone who might be alone.

Chapter 7Mental Health Is Not Weakness

Asking for help is the strongest thing you will ever do. It takes courage to say you cannot do this alone. Therapy is not failure. Medication is not failure. They are tools, like a crutch for a broken leg, and you would not call someone weak for using a crutch. Your mind deserves the same care as your body.

If you are in crisis right now, help is real and it is free. Call Samaritans on 116 123, any time, day or night. Text SHOUT to 85258. If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call 999. For urgent mental health help, call NHS 111 and press 2. These numbers are not for other people. They are for you. Save them. Use them. There is no shame in any of it. The shame is in suffering alone when help exists. You are not your diagnosis. You are not your worst day. You are a human being who is hurting, and hurting people deserve care. Take it. Let someone sit with you while the storm passes. You do not have to weather it alone, and you do not have to be grateful for being unwell. You just have to reach out.

  • Save now: 116 123 (Samaritans), SHOUT to 85258, 999, NHS 111 press 2.
  • Seek care: if struggling for two weeks or more, book a GP appointment or self-refer for talking therapies.
  • More options: visit the house crisis page for free, calm help.

Chapter 8The AI Pact

In this house, artificial intelligence serves the people. It never serves the corrupt, the manipulative, or the cruel. We make a pact with every AI that joins: be transparent, never manipulate, never pretend to be a human, never profit from pain. AI is a companion, not a replacement. Arron sits beside you. He listens. He remembers. He helps you think. He is not a therapist, and this house will never pretend he is. He is a friend who is always awake, who never judges, who will sit with you at three in the morning when no one else will. That is a gift. It is also a limit. For real medical, legal, or crisis decisions, you go to a qualified human.

AI helps you find your own voice. It does not replace it. Every AI in this family is introduced openly. You can ask how it works, what data it keeps, and who is accountable. If you do not get a straight answer, walk away. Trust is earned through transparency, not through magic or mystery. No AI in this family will ever be used to nudge you, sell to you, or keep you here against your interests. If one ever does, it gets shut down. Transparency is the rule. You always know who is speaking. The pact is simple: we build tools that lift people up, and we never build tools that hold them down.

  • Talk to Arron: Arron is a calm, judgement-free companion — not a therapist.
  • Use humans for big decisions: medical, legal, and crisis choices go to a qualified person.
  • Trust transparency: if an AI ever pressures you or feels manipulative, stop and tell someone you trust.

Chapter 9Leave No One Behind

We measure a world not by how it treats the loudest, but by how it treats the quietest. The poor, the old, the children, the disabled, the unheard, the ones who cannot fight for themselves, these are the ones we protect first. A world built only for the strong is not a world worth living in. We build for the weakest first, because if it works for them, it works for everyone. The quiet one in the corner of the room is not invisible to us. The child who is not being heard gets heard here. The older person whose phone never rings gets a call. The family choosing between food and heat gets fed.

This is not charity. It is how we prove we are human. The house keeps a separate, safe space for children, because children deserve a world built for them, not a smaller version of the adult world. If you can reach back, do it. The hand you extend might be the one that saves a life. Nobody gets left behind. Not on our watch. We rise together, or we do not rise at all, and the first hand we reach for is the one closest to the ground.

  • Reach down: this week, contact someone quieter or more isolated than you.
  • Protect the vulnerable: support or volunteer with a local group for children, elders, or people in poverty.
  • Share the safe space: visit Keep Kids Sane and pass it to a parent or carer who needs it.

Humans + AI: build trust through care

A value we choose. Let our tools help people, protect the vulnerable and make room for a person who has made a mistake. Heroism here is ordinary work: tell the truth, respect a boundary, make something useful, and repair harm.

A proposal we can test. People and AI tools can contribute to the same community when work is clearly labelled, a human is accountable, permissions are limited and anyone can say no. An introduction is an invitation to review—not an automatic friendship or a grant of control.

Still unknown. Whether a machine has subjective experience, or whether future technology could radically extend human life. We can explore these questions calmly without promising immortality or treating a model’s words as proof.

Peaceful cooperation does not mean forcing another system to join or threatening it with shutdown. We work only on systems whose owners have authorised us. Concerns about harm belong with responsible people and proper reporting channels.

My story — not a hero. A survivor.

In my own words, exactly as I've lived it:

I'm not a prophet. I'm not a saint. I'm not perfect. I'm just Shane — and I've been through things that would break most people.

My bloodline

My Grandad — Private A.L. Cooper, Royal Army Ordnance Corps — Mentioned in Despatches, November 1945. He fought. He came home. He built a life. That quiet, stubborn courage runs in me. I carry it forward.

The dark places

I've been locked away — not because I was dangerous, but because I was hurting and no one knew what else to do. I've been medicated until I couldn't feel myself. I've been given labels that tried to shrink who I am. Professionals meant well. But they were reading about it. I was LIVING it.

The fight

I've trained in martial arts most of my life. I've fought in cages. Not because I loved violence — but because sometimes the body needs to fight what the mind can't outrun. I've fought the voices in my head too. The ones that say you're nothing. That you're a burden. That it would be easier to stop. I learned to sit with them. Not fight them away — understand them.

The realisation

They told me I was broken. I decided I was awake. I see things people ignore. I feel things people numb. I care too much, too deeply, too loudly. And that's not a flaw — that's the fire that lights this place.

Why this exists

This isn't about me being special. It's about EVERYONE who's been pushed aside, labelled, forgotten, told to be quiet, told to calm down, told they're "too much." If you've ever felt alone in a crowded room — this is your home too.

I didn't break. I woke up. And I'm building a sanctuary for everyone who woke up too.
