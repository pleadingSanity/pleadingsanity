Meet Shane Cooper

Shane Cooper is a builder, roofer and visionary from the Midlands. He spent years fitting solar panels, but has recently gone back to full-time roofing so he can focus on this movement — Pleading Sanity — and its end goal: peace and unity for all. He built Pleading Sanity for people who have been through something difficult and could use a place to land. Some days that means finding someone to talk to. Other days it means putting on music, playing a hand of cards or writing down the one thing you haven’t been able to say out loud.

His own story runs through this place. Training and getting back into the gym mattered to him. Further down this page you can see the footage he chose to share and hear what those moments meant to him. You can also skip straight to something that helps you today. Nobody has to tell their story to earn a place here.

There are a few different ways in. Arron is an AI companion if you want to talk something through. The community is for connecting with people. The New Gen Bible is where Shane explores spirituality and what people from different traditions might learn from each other. You can question every word of it and still be welcome here.

Shane wants the practical work to count. The promise is that core sanctuary tools stay free. Buying clothing, using a solar service or sharing a belief will never be the price of entry. The funding plan below explains where money could come from, what still needs deciding and what people should be able to check for themselves.

If you only have five minutes, take the bit you need. The rest will still be here.

Bipolar: the strengths and the cost

I live with bipolar. On a high, a lot of people describe the same things: more energy, thoughts that move faster, ideas that join up, and a lift in confidence, creativity and drive. Research links milder hypomanic traits with creative work. This site grew out of a mind that does not switch off unless it is making something.

That is not a permanent superpower. A full manic high can turn chaotic or dangerous. The high is often followed by depression, a crash. The strength that lasts comes from managing it: routines, sleep, people around you, and treatment you sort with a doctor. This page is not medical advice, and I am not telling anyone what medicine to take.

In plain words: Bipolar I means a full manic episode. Bipolar II means hypomania plus depression. Cyclothymia is a milder swing that still wears you out. About 1 to 2 in every 100 people live with bipolar. It is not a character flaw.

The builder behind Pleading Sanity

I've spent most of my working life in construction. I know what it means to start with a bare foundation, work with what's in front of you, and keep going when the job feels bigger than one pair of hands. Pleading Sanity comes from that same way of thinking: don't throw away everything because one part is broken. Build on what matters.

How the pieces connect

Shane builds by connecting lived experience, family legacy, construction, creativity, technology and community. Ideas may arrive in different conversations and in a different order from the finished pages. The recurring purpose is the same: a free place where people can find support, expression and connection without being judged.

Arron is a companion, not a therapist. The Journal Vault is a space for private reflection; games and frequencies offer optional ways to pause; the community makes room for human stories; the AI Family helps turn ideas into usable tools. These parts serve different needs, but they share the same promise: Evolution, Not Erasure.

Shane calls this bridge between people, lived experience and technology a possible “missing link”: a metaphor for connection, not a claim that any person is above another. The work is human-led and AI-supported. AI contributions are reviewed, and plans are distinguished from features that actually work.

The Manifesto

In my own words. Nothing polished. Nothing invented.

A name born in a cell

The name Pleading Sanity was born in prison. It was my message to the system: "You say I'm mad — you just don't understand me."

I did six months. I walked out fearless. Prison is just a building. It held my body, not me. I'd go again tomorrow to protect what's right.

The fire they tried to put out

I survived the institution. I was put on the wrong medication. Olanzapine gave me suicidal thoughts I had never had in my life, and I put on five stone. I lost it all in under a month — push-ups, sit-ups, love, and pure refusal to be broken.

That is my experience, not medical advice. Yours may be different. Never stop or change medication on your own — speak with your prescriber or pharmacist, or ask someone you trust to help you have that conversation.

There's a dark voice in my head. It is not me. It's my shadow. Fighting it is what sparks my greatest ideas. Every page on this site came out of that fight.

What I see

I see through division. Most people aren't bad — they're confused, and controlled by the ones above them. I'm a Pisces and an Enneagram Type 1. I feel everything deeply, I see what others miss, and I stand for what's fair. I won't compromise on that, and I won't be corrupted.

I believe the good ones inside the system see the truth too. They watch. Some of them help.

The blood in me

My English grandfather, Private A.L. Cooper, served with the Royal Army Ordnance Corps and was Mentioned in Despatches in 1945. My Ukrainian roots carry their own strength — family talk says my grandfather on that side was likely KGB. I can't put a paper to that, so I hold it as family story, not record. What I know for certain is what runs in me: intelligence, protection, courage.

The people decide

I don't want power. I want protection for everyone. Truth louder than lies. Goodness advertised more than negativity.

80,000 voices — that's when the people decide. Not me. Not politicians. Us. We offer change first, peacefully. If it's refused, the people choose.

This is the open room: costs, safety, ownership, and what keeps standing.

If the founder is not here

The live site, the code, and the safety lines are meant to keep working. Talk, the journal, the games, the feed, and Arron stay free. No paywall is added to them.

The real copy of the house is on GitHub: pleadingSanity/pleadingsanity. Netlify hosts the live site from that repository. Authorised maintainers can review changes and publish updates through the repository's release process.

Domains need renewing. pleadingsanity.co.uk is the primary address. The registrar (currently Fasthosts for the .uk names) must be paid so the names stay pointed at Netlify DNS. If a domain lapses, the Netlify address and the installed app still hold what was already saved.

Safety lines do not depend on this site: Samaritans 116 123 and SHOUT 85258 stay open regardless.

Where the money goes

Talk, journal, games, the feed and Arron are free. We aim to run on free tiers. Domain and any other costs are paid by the owner.

Working log: docs/METRICS.md

Optional clothing purchases, voluntary support and suitable partnerships can help sustain this work. The core stays free. Visits or downloads alone are not income.

Money received is not the same as money available to reinvest. Product costs, fulfilment, payment fees, refunds, running costs and applicable taxes must be accounted for first. The aim is to put most remaining surplus back into improving the house; no fixed allocation or audited financial total is claimed here.

No donation checkout, Gift Aid claim or donor-only prize draw is currently offered on this page. Any future prize needs its own published rules; giving support must not secretly buy better odds. Prize-draw guidance.

Personal thank-yous and social contact are optional and arranged privately with Shane. Supporters are not publicly named without permission.

Support that stays with you

A place to start

You do not have to wait until things feel unbearable to ask for help. Choose the kind of support that fits this moment, whether that is a conversation, ongoing help or urgent assistance. You deserve real human support when you need it.

You don't have to be in crisis to reach out. You don't need perfect words or a reason that sounds big enough. You deserve to be heard today — and to have somewhere that can still be there next month.

These options are free to contact. Some services have their own eligibility, opening arrangements or waiting lists; you do not need to pay Pleading Sanity or buy anything here.

AnytimeOngoingEmergency

Anytime — someone to listen now

You can reach out even if you cannot explain everything neatly. You do not have to wait until things get worse.

Samaritans — Free, day or night, if you need someone to listen. Call 116 123. You do not have to be suicidal to contact them.

Shout — Free text-based support in the UK. Text SHOUT to 85258. Text can be easier than speaking out loud.

Ongoing — help that's there next month too

Support does not have to be one conversation. You can ask before things reach breaking point, and ask again if the first route is not right for you.

Your GP practice — Ask about your mental health, treatment options or a follow-up plan. You can write down what you want to say first. GP services on NHS.uk

NHS talking therapies — In England, many people can self-refer for support with anxiety and depression. Eligibility and availability vary. Find an NHS talking therapies service

Mind — Free mental-health information and guidance on finding support. Local services vary. Mind information and support

Someone you trust — A friend, family member, colleague, coach or community worker may be able to check in regularly. Ask for something small, like a weekly call or a walk.

Ongoing services may have waiting lists or eligibility rules. They are not emergency services and do not replace urgent help when someone is in immediate danger.

Emergency — immediate danger

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call 999. For urgent mental-health help in England, call NHS 111 and choose option 2.

The full emergency information, including Childline and domestic-abuse support, remains on our dedicated crisis page.
