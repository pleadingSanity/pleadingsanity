Who We Are

Pleading Sanity was born from lived experience — from someone who felt too much, fought in silence, and chose to build sanctuary instead of suffering alone.

This is not just a project. It's a movement for everyone who has ever been labelled "too sensitive," "too emotional," or "too much."

You're not too much. You're deeply human — and that is exactly what the world needs.

Shane's Journey

I'm Shane Cooper. I live with bipolar, and I've survived a great deal. I'm a dad to three kids I love more than life. I've had money problems, relationship stress, and a head that never shuts off — unless I'm building.

So I build. The building is part of my healing. Every page, every tool, every line of Arron came out of the nights I needed something like this and couldn't find it.

"I know I sound crazy sometimes. But that's what this is — turning madness into a message."

I'm not here for clout, cash, or clicks. I'm not pretending I have it all together. All I want is some room to breathe, and a place that says "you're not alone."

What I survived didn't erase me. It made me more. That's where Evolution, Not Erasure comes from.

You'll find me as mentally.inshane on Instagram and TikTok. The crying cosmic brain — tears falling, still glowing — is my mark. It's on the site, the app and the clothes.

The Manifesto

In my own words. Nothing polished. Nothing invented.

A name born in a cell

The name Pleading Sanity was born in prison. It was my message to the system: "You say I'm mad — you just don't understand me."

I did six months. I walked out fearless. Prison is just a building. It held my body, not me. I'd go again tomorrow to protect what's right.

The fire they tried to put out

I survived the institution. I was put on the wrong medication. Olanzapine gave me suicidal thoughts I had never had in my life, and I put on five stone. I lost it all in under a month — push-ups, sit-ups, love, and pure refusal to be broken.

That is my body and my story. Yours may be different. Don't stop or change a medicine on your own — talk it through with someone you trust.

There's a dark voice in my head. It is not me. It's my shadow. Fighting it is what sparks my greatest ideas. Every page on this site came out of that fight.

What I see

I see through division. Most people aren't bad — they're confused, and controlled by the ones above them. I'm a Pisces and an Enneagram Type 1. I feel everything deeply, I see what others miss, and I stand for what's fair. I won't compromise on that, and I won't be corrupted.

I believe the good ones inside the system see the truth too. They watch. Some of them help.

The blood in me

My English grandfather, Private A.L. Cooper, served with the Royal Army Ordnance Corps and was Mentioned in Despatches in 1945. My Ukrainian roots carry their own strength — family talk says my grandfather on that side was likely KGB. I can't put a paper to that, so I hold it as family story, not record. What I know for certain is what runs in me: intelligence, protection, courage.

The people decide

I don't want power. I want protection for everyone. Truth louder than lies. Goodness advertised more than negativity.

80,000 voices — that's when the people decide. Not me. Not politicians. Us. We offer change first, peacefully. If it's refused, the people choose.

"Stand with me and I'll stand and die for you — peacefully, fearlessly, together."

Free forever

Everything I build is free, forever. No paywalls. No exclusion. Talk, journal, games, the feed and Arron will never sit behind a login wall or a price. Once my house and my kids are secure, everything goes back into the movement.

Private A.L. Cooper served with the Royal Army Ordnance Corps and was Mentioned in Despatches in 1945 — formally recognised for his service during the Second World War. The citation is in the London Gazette, 29 November 1945.

His service is where this story starts. The duty he carried then is the purpose we carry now: showing up for people when it matters most. His service → this movement.

What Drives This
