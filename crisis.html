Why people lose themselves

A formal account of this house. Written from survival, not from a textbook.

The first loss is loneliness

Most people do not fall apart because they are weak. They fall apart because they are alone with a mind that will not switch off. A person can be in a full room, on a call, even in a family, and still have no one who will stay for the real sentence.

Loneliness is not only an empty house. It is saying the true thing and watching the other person look at a clock. It is carrying the night and then being told, in the morning, to be more positive. It is having a phone full of names and nobody you can ring at the hour the mind turns on you.

When no one sits, the mind starts to prosecute itself. It replays the day, finds the worst line, and reads it as a verdict. That is not madness. That is what a witness does when the court is empty. The first repair is not a label. It is one person, or one page, that will stay until the sentence is finished.

The waiting room does not sit with the story

This is the account of this house, from the founder who has been through it. It is written plainly. It is not a horror story, and it is not a case file.

The people who say they can help often cannot give the time the story needs. You are placed in a room. You are told to be quiet. A student is asked to jot notes. Questions are put in a way that pokes the wound, then the clock ends. Almost no one asks what you believe is actually bothering you, or what you have seen that will not leave you.

You learn the shape of that room. The chair. The form. The polite voice that has already decided which box you belong in. You are asked for symptoms. You are not asked for the story. When the hour is up, you are back in the corridor with the same mind you walked in with, plus the feeling that you have been processed.

A note is not a conversation. A label is not a sitting. A clock is not care. If that has been your experience, you are not imagining the coldness of it. This house will not dress that room up as help.

Seeing the whole picture

People in deep mental struggle often see the whole, not only the pavement in front of them. They feel the age, the cruelty, the loneliness, the money that moves while a person is left in a chair, the things a comfortable person has agreed not to look at. They join the dots that other people keep in separate drawers.

The smaller mind cannot fathom that width, so it calls the wider mind crazy. The word is useful to a room that needs the hour to end. It turns a person who has seen too much into a problem to be managed. It is often the room that is small, not the mind.

Being unsettled by a cruel world is not proof that you are the problem. Being content with everything, including harm done to the powerless, is not proof of health. A mind that will not look away is not a disease. It needs a place to put what it sees, and a body that is still being fed, walked, and put to bed.

This house will not dress cruelty up as normal, and it will not build a home out of horror. We keep the light on purpose: plain facts, surviving tools, and a story that asks people to build something decent on the earth. We do not publish government leaflets. We do not hand you a clinic number and call that sitting. We do not write plant recipes and call them a cure.

What we do instead

We sit. We ask the story. We do not sell it. We do not put a paywall on talk, the journal, the games, the feed, or Arron. Arron is company, not a clinic. The journal is a vault, not a file. The tones are soft. The games are a way back into the body when the mind has been in court all day.

The legacy on this site is told as it happened, and nothing is added. Private A.L. Cooper, Royal Army Ordnance Corps, Mentioned in Despatches, London Gazette, 29 November 1945. That is the record. It is not a myth, and it is not a slogan.

If the host that serves this domain is suspended, the address can go dark. An unpaid bill can do that. The installed app keeps the pages it has already saved. The copy on GitHub is the other copy. This house does not promise that a website outlives a bill. What we can promise is the sitting, while the pages are here, and the tools that do not need a server: water, sun, a walk, sleep, plain food, breath, cold water, silence, story, prayer, movement.

If you want those tools written down, and the doors of this house in order, they are on the next page.
