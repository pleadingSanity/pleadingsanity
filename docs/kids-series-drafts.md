# Keep Kids Sane — Pilot Scripts & Visual Notes
**Status:** DRAFT · Ages 3–9 · Not filmed · Not uploaded  
**Characters:** Ollie the Owl, Zara the Squirrel, Max, Mia, Blinky the Robot  
**House rule:** Funny, kind, real science, no pressure to be perfect. Evolution Not Erasure.

## Visual identity notes
- Palette: deepest night `#000103`, soft cyan `#00fff0`, gentle purple accents, frost text.
- Style: rounded shapes, big eyes, soft lighting, cosmic night sky background that stays calm.
- Ollie: round owl, big curious eyes, small notebook.
- Zara: energetic squirrel with a tool belt and a slightly messy tail.
- Max: short, sturdy, wears a simple cape that sometimes gets in the way.
- Mia: calm, holds a small lantern or soft light.
- Blinky: small round robot, one big blinking light, friendly beeps.

## Episode 1 — The Big Breath Balloon (Pilot)
**Learning:** Air takes up space. Slowing down helps.
**Beats:**
1. Ollie introduces the Great Sky Festival. Everyone is excited.
2. Zara builds a balloon. Max wants to launch immediately. Blinky starts a countdown too soon.
3. Balloon falls flat. Faces show disappointment, not shame.
4. Mia says “Let’s stop and notice our breath.” Soft breathing together (3 slow breaths).
5. Ollie explains simply: air fills the balloon. They find a small leak.
6. They fix it, try again, and cheer the teamwork more than the height.
7. Closing song line: “One breath, one try, one friend at a time.”

## Episode 2 — Zara’s Brilliant Mistake
**Learning:** Predictions and testing. Mistakes can teach.
**Beats:**
1. Zara shows a new invention that should stack blocks higher.
2. It tumbles. She feels embarrassed. Max wants to fix it fast.
3. Mia suggests “one change at a time.”
4. They test: base wider → better. Blocks lighter → better. They write simple notes.
5. It works enough. They celebrate the learning, not a perfect win.
6. Blinky beeps “Mistake = data!” Everyone laughs.

## Episode 3 — Blinky’s Feelings Decoder
**Learning:** Signals and senses. Listening comes first.
**Beats:**
1. Blinky builds a “feelings calculator.” He points it at friends and guesses wrong.
2. Max is frustrated. Zara is excited but the machine says “sad.”
3. Mia shows that faces and bodies give clues, but asking “How do you feel?” is clearer.
4. They turn the machine into a “listening light” that only lights up when someone speaks.
5. Everyone shares one real feeling. Blinky learns questions work better than guesses.
6. Closing: the light stays soft and open.
