Insane Solar · Plain English Energy

Get Off the Mains If You Want

Solar panels and a battery can cut your bills, keep the lights on in a cut, and take pressure off the grid. You do not have to go fully off-grid — most homes stay connected and sell spare power back. This page explains how it works, what it costs, and the rules that keep it safe and legal.

How solar + battery storage works

The journey of the electricity is simple. Panels make DC power in daylight, the inverter turns it into the AC power your home uses, and any surplus is stored in a battery for the evening. If you stay connected to the grid, extra power can be exported and you get paid under the Smart Export Guarantee (SEG).

Your home
  • Grid-tied (most homes): you stay connected. You use solar first, then battery, then grid. Surplus is exported and paid via SEG. You are never without power because the grid is the backup.
  • Off-grid (fully): no grid connection at all — you need enough panels and battery (and often a generator) to cover the darkest winter days. It is a bigger, more expensive step and usually only makes sense for remote buildings.
  • Hybrid: grid-tied with a large battery so you can ride through power cuts and use almost no grid power on sunny days.

Battery systems — a few well-known names

These are examples only, not recommendations or affiliate links. Compare warranties, usable capacity (kWh), round-trip efficiency and installer support before buying. A good installer will size the battery to your actual use, not just sell the biggest one.

Tesla Powerwall

13.5 kWh home battery with app monitoring and backup during cuts. One of the premium-priced options; often paired with Tesla solar or any compatible inverter.

GivEnergy

UK-popular hybrid inverters and batteries (roughly 5–13 kWh), strong app control and a wide installer network. Often a mid-price choice.

Anker SOLIX

Plug-and-play balcony and home battery systems, plus larger whole-home storage. Known for portable power stations expanding into fixed home storage.

EcoFlow

Portable power stations and modular home battery kits (PowerOcean / Delta series) for backup and partial off-grid setups. Good for renters and smaller systems.

Brand facts are general product descriptions. Always confirm current specs and price with the manufacturer or an MCS-certified installer.

Figures are typical ranges and change with equipment, roof type and installer. VAT on residential solar panels and battery storage installed together is currently 0% (a relief running to 31 March 2027). Always get at least three quotes.

  • Smart Export Guarantee (SEG): suppliers with 150,000+ customers must pay you for exported electricity. In 2024–25 the average export rate was around 10–13p per kWh, but rates vary widely (from ~1p up to higher tied tariffs) — shop around. You need an MCS certificate and a smart meter to be paid.
  • Running costs: panels and inverters have almost no running cost; batteries last roughly 10–15 years (check the warranty's cycle count). Inverters are usually replaced once in the system's life.
  • Grid strain: every kWh you make and use at home is a kWh the grid does not have to deliver — batteries smooth out peaks, which is why the grid benefits too.

Practical and legal: do it right the first time

  • Use an MCS-certified installer. The Microgeneration Certification Scheme (MCS) certificate is required to claim SEG payments and most warranties. A rogue installer without MCS can leave you with no export payments and no comeback.
  • Building Regulations Part P (electrical safety). Connecting a generator to your home is notifiable electrical work. An MCS installer registered with a competent person scheme (NICEIC / NAPIT) self-certifies and notifies building control for you. If they cannot, building control must be involved separately.
  • Notify the Distribution Network Operator (DNO). For systems up to 3.68 kW per phase the installer follows the G98 "connect and notify" route (notify within 28 days). Larger systems need G99 approval before energising. Your installer handles this — confirm it is in their quote.
  • Sign up for a SEG tariff. After install, shop SEG rates and register with a supplier; you need the MCS certificate and a smart meter (or export meter).
  • Renters: you usually need the landlord's permission for panels on the building. Portable plug-in solar (balcony) systems may be possible — check your tenancy agreement and ask.
